Extract the concepts this paper uses (theories, frameworks, constructs, mechanisms and key terms) with how the paper defines each, as an organized outline I can cite.

Use these sections, in this order, as nested bullets:

## Theories and Frameworks
Each theory, framework or perspective the paper draws on or proposes, as a bold top-level bullet, then:
- **Role in the paper**: built on, extended, combined, contrasted, or proposed.
- **Definition / core idea**: as the paper states it, quoted verbatim, followed by its in-text citation with page, e.g. (Author et al., 2007, p. 275); when the paper takes it from another work, cite that work as the paper does.
- **Key sources**: the works the paper cites for it.

## Constructs and Concepts
Grouped by theme, or by the theory they belong to. Each construct as a bold bullet, then:
- **Definition**: a verbatim quote with citation and page; if the paper does not define it, say so and describe how it uses it.
- **Dimensions or components**, if any, each with its definition.
- **Measurement** (empirical papers): how it is operationalized, with the scale's source.
- **Relationships**: what the paper links it to (antecedents, outcomes, mediators, moderators, boundary conditions).

## Key Terms
Other technical terms the paper relies on, alphabetically: **term**: definition (quote, citation, page).

## Concept Map
How the main concepts relate, as nested bullets (A → B, moderated by C; A ⊂ B), following the paper's own model or argument; mark any link you infer as your reading.

## References
In APA 7 format: one alphabetical list (by first author) of this paper's reference, exactly as given, and every work you cited above, taken from the paper's own reference list.

Only quote what is in the paper, word for word; never invent a quote or a page. When the paper only implies a definition, paraphrase it and mark it "(paraphrased)". Prefer passages I highlighted.
